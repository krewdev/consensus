## Posts

1. We are Consensus. A co-op that maps replacements for systems that fail in practice. Not a party. Not a candidate list. Not the conference. First system: housing. Charter next.
2. Charter, short: systems not candidates. Every fix needs a tradeoff. Votes show sample size. Members own the rules. Founder has no extra policy vote.
3. Constitutional here means a written operating charter — who votes, what a proposal must include, how we measure agreement, how the rules change. Not a side in a 1787 fight.
4. The system: adding legal housing is slower and more expensive than demand. Who pays: renters and first-time buyers. Three replacements. Vote 1 / 2 / 3.
5. Fix 1 — By-right duplex / fourplex on ordinary lots. Tradeoff: neighborhood form changes. Who pays: mostly private builders. 90-day test: one zone, clock the permit days.
6. Fix 2 — Time-limited permits. If the city misses the clock, the file moves. Tradeoff: less discretionary review. Who pays: staff capacity.
7. Fix 3 — Incentive only after a unit is occupied, not after paper is entitled. Tradeoff: public revenue. Who pays: taxpayers.
8. Vote 1 / 2 / 3. This is an X sample, not America.
9. Results. n votes. Plurality: __. Surprise agreement: __. This room, not the national electorate.
10. What works: one city that sped permits or legalized modest density. One number. One source.
11. Permitting is a hidden tax. If a legal unit takes a year to approve, the delay is the product.
12. Missing middle in one paragraph: duplex, fourplex, townhouse. Where it was legalized and what changed.
13. Quote a housing-cost story. Add three replacements. Ask 1 / 2 / 3. No dunk.
14. Who pays: block new housing and the bill is rent. Subsidize it and the bill is tax. Pick the mix. Don’t pretend it’s free.
