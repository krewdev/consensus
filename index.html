This week: housing supply

The system: legal housing is slower and more expensive to add than demand. Who pays: renters and first-time buyers. Vote one replacement. This is this room, not the national electorate.

1 — By-right duplex / fourplex

Allow modest density on ordinary lots without a special hearing.

Tradeoff: neighborhood form changes. Who pays: mostly private builders. 90-day test: one zone, clock the permit days.

2 — Permit shot clock

If the city misses the deadline, the file moves.

Tradeoff: less discretionary review. Who pays: staff capacity. 90-day test: publish average days-to-decision.

3 — Pay after occupancy

Incentive only after a unit is occupied, not after paper is entitled.

Tradeoff: public revenue. Who pays: taxpayers. 90-day test: credit only with certificate of occupancy.

Charter v0.1

Constitutional here means a written operating charter: who votes, what a proposal must include, how agreement is measured, how the rules change. Not a side in a 1787 fight.

  1. Purpose. Map replacements for failing systems. Publish the tradeoffs.
  2. Scope. Systems and mechanisms only. No candidate pages, party rankings, or GOTV.
  3. Membership. One person, one vote. Founder, donors, and staff get no extra policy vote.
  4. Issue format. Problem, who pays today, at least three replacement cards with tradeoff, payer, 90-day test, and where tried.
  5. Voting. Agree / disagree / pass, or rank. No reply-war as the product.
  6. Honesty. Always show sample size. Never call a site poll “the American electorate.”
  7. Consensus rule. Publish as consensus only what survives across opposing clusters. Plurality is reported separately.
  8. Amendment. Charter changes need a higher bar than ordinary issue votes. Post the text 7 days prior.
  9. Money and speech. No endorsing, opposing, or donating to candidates or parties. No token. No voter file.
  10. Capture. No party, PAC, or single donor sets the agenda. Agenda is member-ranked. Sources required on featured briefs.
